# Extra ELA Quiz Practice — Parent Answer Key

Original practice for the October 23 quiz skills. This is not the teacher’s test or answer key. Answer choices rotate on screen: match the answer text, not a letter. Keep this key closed during independent work.

## Quiz Practice A: Evidence, Summary & Media

**1. Which claim best accounts for the essay’s ending as well as the girl’s experience?**

- Reading partnerships can help both people and animals.

Paragraph 6 illustrates a child’s progress; paragraph 8 explains benefits for shelter animals, and the ending brings the benefits together.

**2. Why does the author first list different reasons children may struggle, then discuss confidence?**

- To move from varied difficulties to the confidence concern the essay emphasizes.

The list establishes several difficulties. The next paragraph focuses the explanation on confidence; that emphasis is not proof that every reader has the same needs.

**3. SELECT TWO: Which details most directly support the claim that a dog can reduce pressure during reading?**

- A dog allows the child to read at a comfortable pace.
- A dog does not laugh when the reader makes a mistake.

Pace and nonjudgmental listening explain reduced pressure. The program’s location and the dog’s breed do not explain that effect.

**4. Which missing idea would most weaken a summary that mentions only children becoming more confident?**

- People reading to shelter animals can help the animals become calmer.

Benefits for shelter animals are a major development. The age, preference, and founding year are background details.

**5. A response says paragraph 6 proves every participant finishes a book. What correction is needed?**

- Treat the girl’s progress as one supporting example, not a universal result.

One participant’s experience illustrates the explanation. It does not establish an outcome for every participant.

**6. Which explanation identifies paragraph 8’s function rather than merely retelling its subject?**

- It extends the explanation by showing animals receiving benefits from human readers.

Function explains the paragraph’s contribution to the whole argument. The other choices name details without explaining that contribution.

**7. Which central idea is best supported?**

- Repeated observation can reveal a pattern while leaving its cause uncertain.

The narrator records a recurring timing pattern but explicitly says more evidence is needed to explain why.

**8. What is paragraph 1’s main function?**

- It establishes an unsuccessful expectation that the later change in method addresses.

The initial expectation and short visits establish a problem; paragraph 2 shows the revised approach.

**9. SELECT TWO: Which details support a claim that the narrator used a more systematic method?**

- The narrator recorded arrival times.
- The narrator continued the same routine for a week.

Written records and a repeated routine provide comparable observations. An expectation or one empty visit does not establish a systematic method.

**10. Why include paragraph 3?**

- To distinguish a recorded pattern from a proven explanation.

The final paragraph limits the conclusion: knowing when a pattern occurs is different from establishing its cause.

**11. Which objective summary is most accurate?**

- After short visits produced little information, the narrator recorded a week of visits, noticed a timing pattern, and recognized that its cause remained uncertain.

The summary includes the difficulty, method, finding, and limitation without praise or unsupported conclusions.

**12. Which statement is supported by the narrator’s actions?**

- The narrator was willing to revise an approach when it produced little evidence.

Staying farther away, remaining longer, and collecting records show a revised approach. They do not establish complete knowledge or control.

**13. Which claim is supported by both sources?**

- People read near shelter dogs.

Both sources include people reading near shelter dogs. Only the article describes several weeks of visitor records, and it does not establish reading as the sole cause.

**14. Which comparison best explains a difference in evidence?**

- The article reports change across weeks; the clip supplies sound and visible behavior from one scene.

Print supplies the longer time span and records. The described video provides audible and visible features of one moment.

**15. What important qualification would be lost if the comparison used only the clip?**

- Changes in care other than reading might also have contributed.

The article explicitly identifies another possible contribution. That limitation is absent from the described clip.

**16. Which statement needs additional evidence?**

- Listening to reading caused every observed improvement.

The sources do not isolate reading as the cause, and the article mentions other changes. The other choices are directly reported or described.

**17. Which response completes a claim–evidence–reasoning connection?**

- It states a claim, uses a relevant detail, and explains why that detail supports the claim

Reasoning is the link between evidence and the claim.

**18. What is the strongest cross-subject use of this ELA skill?**

- Use precise evidence and explanation when analyzing a Science phenomenon or a Social Studies source

Evidence habits transfer, but the reasoning must remain subject-accurate.

## Quiz Practice B: Mixed Challenge

**1. Which group is an independent clause?**

- The observers returned before noon

The observers is the subject and returned is the verb; the group expresses a complete thought. The although and because groups are dependent; the noun phrase lacks a verb.

**2. Which group is a dependent clause?**

- Unless the reader checks the passage

Unless the reader checks the passage has a subject and verb but leaves a condition unfinished. The beside and checking groups are phrases.

**3. In “Although the first observation was brief, the later records revealed a pattern,” which is the independent clause?**

- the later records revealed a pattern

The later records revealed a pattern is a complete thought. Although makes the other clause dependent.

**4. SELECT TWO: Which groups are clauses rather than phrases?**

- when the reader tried again
- the dog settled beside the chair

When the reader tried again and the dog settled beside the chair each contain a subject and verb. One is dependent and one independent; both are clauses.

**5. Which revision repairs the fragment “Because the observer needed more evidence” while keeping the cause-and-effect relationship?**

- Because the observer needed more evidence, she returned to the pond.

The dependent reason clause joins an independent result clause, with a comma after the introductory clause. A period after the dependent clause leaves a fragment.

**6. A student says “After the observation” and “After the observation ended” are both dependent clauses. Which correction is accurate?**

- The first is a phrase; the second has a subject and verb and is a dependent clause.

The observation ended supplies a subject-verb pair in the second group. The first has no subject-verb pair. After introduces dependence only when used with a clause here.

**7. Which sentence correctly places a dependent clause first?**

- When the reader paused, the dog remained still.

The entire introductory clause is When the reader paused. The comma belongs at its end, not between its subject and verb or inside the main clause.

**8. SELECT TWO: Which groups could each stand alone as a sentence?**

- the evidence supported the claim
- the listener remained patient

The evidence supported the claim and the listener remained patient express complete thoughts. Because and although make the other groups dependent.

**9. Which pair most strongly supports the essay’s idea that help can go both ways?**

- The girl finishes a book; reading helps shelter animals become more comfortable around people.

The pair supplies a benefit for a child and a benefit for animals. Background facts alone do not establish mutual benefit.

**10. Which statement best describes the author’s overall purpose?**

- To explain how reading relationships can benefit children and animals.

The essay develops an informative explanation with examples of both kinds of benefit. It does not give adoption orders or establish universal causes.

**11. Which detail is least important in a brief objective summary?**

- The initial visits lasted exactly ten minutes.

The exact length of the early visits is less central than the problem, changed method, result, and limitation. A summary can say the visits were short without retaining the exact number.

**12. Which claim overstates the evidence?**

- The birds arrived because sunlight warmed the bank.

The passage reports timing but explicitly says its cause was not established. The causal claim goes beyond that evidence.

**13. Which sentence best explains how paragraph 2 develops paragraph 1?**

- It describes a revised method that addresses the earlier lack of observations.

The first paragraph establishes unsuccessful short visits. The second explains a quieter, longer, repeated recording routine.

**14. Why does the first-person viewpoint matter?**

- It gives access to the observer’s expectations and revised thinking.

The narrator reports personal expectations and reasoning. First-person access does not guarantee accuracy or reveal another organism’s thoughts.

**15. SELECT TWO: Which features come directly from the described video rather than the article’s reported records?**

- The sound of a quiet reading voice
- A dog visibly settling near the reader

Voice and visible settling are features of the clip. The records and caution belong to the article.

**16. Which objective summary of the pair stays within the evidence?**

- The article reports changes during reading sessions and notes other possible influences; the clip shows one calm interaction.

This summary distinguishes the article’s time span and qualification from the clip’s single scene without adding a causal guarantee or personal opinion.

## Six written responses: models and checking criteria

Equivalent accurate wording is acceptable. Check evidence and reasoning rather than memorized sentences. These models appear only in this parent key, not in the independent question screen.

**A1 — Reading Buddies summary**

Model: The essay explains how reading to animal listeners can help children feel less anxious and practice more confidently. It also describes how people reading to shelter animals can help the animals become calmer and more comfortable around people.

Check: both kinds of benefit; no opinion; no guarantee for every child or animal; no unnecessary age, breed, or founding-year detail.

**A2 — Heron account claim, two details, reasoning**

Model: The narrator becomes more systematic when the first approach produces little evidence. Recording arrival times creates a record that can be compared instead of relying on memory. Repeating the routine for a week supplies several observations, making it possible to identify a pattern rather than depend on one visit.

Check: a defensible claim; two different precise details; an explanation connecting each detail to the claim. Accept other supported claims, such as willingness to revise an approach. Do not accept a claim that the narrator established the cause of arrival times.

**A3 — Print/media comparison**

Model: Both sources show people reading near shelter dogs. The article describes staff records across six weeks, including several dogs approaching visitors more often. The clip lets the viewer hear a quiet voice and see one dog settle near a reader. The article supplies change over time and a caution about other influences, while the clip makes one interaction directly visible and audible.

Check: shared idea; one correct detail from each; explanation of how the forms differ. Do not accept the clip as evidence of a six-week trend or a guarantee that reading alone caused change.

**A4 — Paragraph function**

Model: Paragraph 2 reports a pattern in the arrival records. Paragraph 3 qualifies that result by explaining that the pattern does not establish its cause. Without paragraph 3, a reader might mistake an observation about timing for proof about why the birds arrive then.

Check: identifies the qualification; relates it specifically to paragraph 2; explains its effect on the reader’s conclusion. Merely saying “it is the last paragraph” is insufficient.

**B1 — Two complex sentences**

Model 1: Although the girl had avoided reading aloud, she finished a book after practicing with a dog.

- Dependent: “Although the girl had avoided reading aloud” — subject: girl; verb phrase: had avoided.
- Independent: “she finished a book after practicing with a dog” — subject: she; verb: finished.
- Comma follows the complete introductory dependent clause. “After practicing with a dog” is a phrase here.

Model 2: Because the narrator recorded arrival times, the notes revealed a pattern.

- Dependent: “Because the narrator recorded arrival times” — subject: narrator; verb: recorded.
- Independent: “the notes revealed a pattern” — subject: notes; verb: revealed.
- Comma follows the complete introductory dependent clause.

Check: one sentence begins Although and one Because; both contain an independent and a dependent clause; each subject and verb is correctly identified; clauses are correctly labeled; comma is in the correct place. Accept other source-accurate sentences.

**B2 — Comparison with an evidence limit**

Model: Both sources describe a shelter reading interaction. The article reports several dogs approaching visitors more often over six weeks, whereas the clip shows one dog approaching a reader and settling nearby. Print presents a longer record and an alternative explanation, while video supplies sound and visible behavior. Neither source establishes that reading alone caused all improvement.

Check: 3–4 sentences; shared idea; evidence from both; effect of medium; one unsupported conclusion clearly identified. This is separately assessed writing, not automatically scored by the multiple-choice result.
