1. Why Egypt grew along the Nile

The Nile flows north toward the Mediterranean Sea. Upper Egypt is upstream in the south. Lower Egypt is downstream in the north, where the river branches into a delta. Floods left fertile silt, and irrigation moved water to crops. When farmers grew more food than people needed right away, the surplus helped support workers with other jobs.

Worked example

Imagine a village stores extra grain. Some people can now make tools or keep records because they can get food from the farmers.

Check your understanding

Which change most directly helps a village support more full-time craftspeople?

  1. More desert beyond the farms
  2. More grain than the village needs right away
  3. Fewer ways to move water to fields
Reveal answer and reasoning

B. More grain than the village needs right away Extra grain feeds people whose main jobs are outside farming.

Trace flooding, farming and surplus · More practice and sources

2. From Two Lands to one kingdom

Upper and Lower Egypt were political regions. Black Land described the fertile Nile land, and Red Land described the surrounding desert. The traditional account credits Menes with unification. Evidence also links Narmer to early unification, and historians are uncertain how the two names relate. The Double Crown joined symbols of rule over both regions.

Worked example

A ruler shown wearing the Double Crown is being pictured as ruler of both regions. The crown alone cannot tell us the precise sequence of events that brought them together.

Check your understanding

What does the Double Crown represent?

  1. Control over both Upper and Lower Egypt
  2. Two kinds of farmland
  3. Two separate rulers sharing one throne
Reveal answer and reasoning

A. Control over both Upper and Lower Egypt The combined crown symbolized rule over the two political regions.

Follow the Two Lands story · More practice and sources

Unification brought Upper and Lower Egypt under one rule. Follow the teacher’s reading for its account of Menes. Historians also discuss Narmer and the evidence associated with early unification. Keep the classroom account and the historical identification question distinct.

Worked example

A royal image can show symbols associated with both regions. That supports a claim about how royal power was presented. The image alone cannot settle every detail of who unified Egypt.

Check your understanding

What changed with unification?

  1. The Nile changed its flow
  2. Upper and Lower Egypt came under one rule
  3. Egypt stopped farming
Reveal answer and reasoning

B. Upper and Lower Egypt came under one rule Unification concerns political rule over both lands.

3. Pharaohs and government

Pharaohs held political and religious authority. Egyptians valued ma’at, the idea of truth, balance, and proper order. A vizier helped supervise government. Other officials organized resources and work, while scribes kept records. This division of government work is called a bureaucracy.

Worked example

To supply a building project, officials could arrange workers and grain deliveries. Scribes could keep track of both so officials knew what had arrived.

Check your understanding

Which task would most directly help officials track a grain tax?

  1. Performing a temple ritual
  2. Carving a crown
  3. Recording how much each village delivers
Reveal answer and reasoning

C. Recording how much each village delivers Written records let officials compare what was owed with what arrived.

Explain how the government worked · More practice and sources

The pharaoh ruled. A vizier helped oversee government, and other officials handled specific jobs. A bureaucracy is a system of officials and offices. It helps turn a ruler’s decisions into tasks people can actually carry out.

Worked example

When the ruler orders grain to be collected, officials can organize deliveries and scribes can record them. The order, the organization, and the record are separate steps.

Check your understanding

  1. An artisan teaches an apprentice
  2. A family prepares dinner
  3. Several government offices manage different parts of tax collection
Reveal answer and reasoning

C. Several government offices manage different parts of tax collection A bureaucracy divides public work among officials with different duties.

4. The three kingdoms

The Old Kingdom is known for major pyramid construction at Giza. The Middle Kingdom began with reunification after a period of division. In the New Kingdom, Egypt expanded its military influence and built major temples. Periods of division separated these kingdoms.

Worked example

A description of a royal tomb cut into rock in the Valley of the Kings points to the New Kingdom. A description of Khufu’s pyramid points to the Old Kingdom.

Check your understanding

Which development best fits the Middle Kingdom?

  1. Khufu commissions the Great Pyramid
  2. Egypt reunifies after a period of division
  3. New Kingdom rulers are buried in the Valley of the Kings
Reveal answer and reasoning

B. Egypt reunifies after a period of division Reunification after division is a defining development of the Middle Kingdom.

Compare the three kingdoms · More practice and sources

The Old Kingdom is strongly associated with pyramid building. The Middle Kingdom followed reunification and renewed central rule. The New Kingdom saw expansion and powerful rulers. A timeline helps you place evidence before trying to explain it.

Worked example

If a source describes reunification after political division, look for evidence of restored central rule. A pyramid or a military campaign would support a different claim.

Check your understanding

  1. New Kingdom and the first Old Kingdom pyramids
  2. Old Kingdom and New Kingdom imperial expansion
  3. Middle Kingdom and renewed unity
Reveal answer and reasoning

C. Middle Kingdom and renewed unity Renewed central rule is a useful way to recognize the Middle Kingdom.

5. Rulers and their contributions

Djoser is linked to the Step Pyramid at Saqqara, traditionally designed by Imhotep. Khufu commissioned the Great Pyramid at Giza. Hatshepsut is known for trade and building, including the expedition to Punt. Thutmose III expanded military influence. Akhenaten promoted worship of the Aten. Ramesses II commissioned monuments including Abu Simbel and led military campaigns.

Worked example

If a ruler ordered a pyramid built, the ruler commissioned it. The people who planned, cut, moved, and set its stones built it.

Check your understanding

Which pair correctly matches a ruler and an accomplishment?

  1. Khufu and the expedition to Punt
  2. Akhenaten and the Step Pyramid
  3. Hatshepsut and the expedition to Punt
Reveal answer and reasoning

C. Hatshepsut and the expedition to Punt Hatshepsut’s expedition to Punt is an example of New Kingdom trade.

Read evidence about rulers · More practice and sources

Tie each ruler to a specific action or artifact. Djoser is associated with the Step Pyramid and Khufu with the Great Pyramid at Giza. New Kingdom evidence includes Hatshepsut’s Punt expedition, Thutmose III’s campaigns, Akhenaten’s religious changes, and Ramesses II’s monuments. Ask what each source was made to show.

Worked example

A temple scene showing goods from Punt can support a claim about exchange during Hatshepsut’s reign. Because royal monuments celebrate rulers, check other evidence before drawing a broader conclusion.

Check your understanding

Which evidence best supports a claim about Hatshepsut’s trade?

  2. A plan of Khufu’s pyramid
  3. A map of the Nile Delta alone
Reveal answer and reasoning

A. A depiction of the expedition to Punt The expedition depicts a specific exchange tied to her reign.

6. The social pyramid and the jobs that kept Egypt running

A social hierarchy ranks people by status or authority. Pharaohs ruled, officials administered, priests led rituals, scribes kept records, soldiers served in the military, and farmers, artisans, and merchants provided food, goods, and trade. Use the teacher’s five-level diagram for exact placements on the assignment. The Who Done It? mystery uses fictional events to help you practice identifying roles.

Worked example

A farmer grows grain, a scribe records it, and an official organizes where it goes. Their jobs have different levels of authority, but each contributes to keeping the storehouse running.

7. Religion and the afterlife

Egyptians generally worshipped many gods. Ra was associated with the sun, Osiris with the afterlife and rebirth, Isis with protection, and Anubis with embalming. Mummification preserved a body as part of preparation for the afterlife. The weighing of the heart connects belief in the afterlife with ma’at.

Worked example

Khufu’s pyramid at Giza and a rock-cut royal tomb in the Valley of the Kings both tell us about burial beliefs. They also show how royal burial places changed over time.

Check your understanding

Which comparison is accurate?

  1. Both sites are known mainly for Old Kingdom pyramids
  2. Giza is known for Old Kingdom pyramids, and the Valley of the Kings for New Kingdom rock-cut royal tombs
  3. Both sites are known mainly for New Kingdom rock-cut tombs
Reveal answer and reasoning

B. Giza is known for Old Kingdom pyramids, and the Valley of the Kings for New Kingdom rock-cut royal tombs The sites represent different periods and ways of building royal tombs.

Link beliefs to daily decisions · More practice and sources

Egyptians honored multiple gods. Beliefs about order, balance and justice, often expressed through ma’at, shaped how people described good rule and proper conduct. Belief in an afterlife also helps explain choices about burial.

Worked example

A tomb object may suggest what people expected the dead to need. An image of a ruler making an offering tells you something different about worship and royal duty.

Check your understanding

What can burial goods help explain?

  1. Which direction the river flows
  2. What people believed the dead might need
  3. The exact population of a city
Reveal answer and reasoning

B. What people believed the dead might need Objects placed with the dead are evidence about beliefs and burial practices.

Separate mummification, tombs and monuments · More practice and sources

Mummification preserved the body. A tomb was a burial place. The pyramids at Giza and the royal tombs in the Valley of the Kings belong to different settings and periods. The Great Sphinx stands at Giza.

Follow the process

In the assigned reading’s example of a royal burial, embalmers removed organs and stored them in canopic jars. They dried the body with natron, then wrapped it in linen. Drying helped preserve the body. Wrapping and placing the mummy in a tomb came later. Belief in an afterlife helps explain why preserving the body mattered.

Try it: What did natron do?

Choose: A. Dried the body. B. Held the organs. C. Wrapped the body.

Reveal answer

A. Natron dried the body. Canopic jars held organs, and linen was used for wrapping.

Source: Assigned Egypt OER, section 6.4, pp. 23–24.

8. Writing and the Rosetta Stone

Hieroglyphs were signs that could represent sounds or words. Scribes could write on papyrus, a material made from a plant. The Rosetta Stone carries the same decree in three scripts and two languages. Scholars used the Greek text to help decipher Egyptian writing.

Worked example

A grain list is a primary source for what someone recorded at the time. An inscription on a tomb might tell you whom the tomb honored. Each answers a different kind of question.

Check your understanding

Why was the Greek text on the Rosetta Stone useful?

  1. It gave scholars a text they could read and compare with Egyptian scripts
  2. It showed that papyrus and hieroglyphs were the same thing
  3. It explained every Egyptian sign in a separate glossary
Reveal answer and reasoning

A. It gave scholars a text they could read and compare with Egyptian scripts The familiar text gave scholars a way to compare scripts.

Use writing and trade as evidence · More practice and sources

Hieroglyphs were one Egyptian writing system. The Rosetta Stone carries a decree in three scripts, including Greek, which helped scholars decipher Egyptian writing. Records help us see how government and exchange worked. Nubia had its own societies and interacted with Egypt through trade and conflict.

Read the writing as evidence

A hieroglyph is a sign. The assigned reading explains that signs can represent sounds or words. Papyrus is a writing material made from a reed. You could also find hieroglyphs carved on temple and tomb walls. The sign carries meaning and the surface holds the writing.

Worked example. You find signs carved on a tomb wall. The carved signs are the writing. The wall is the surface. If you want to know whom the tomb honored, you need evidence from what the signs say.

Try it: Which is a writing material?

Choose: A. A hieroglyph. B. Papyrus. C. A sound represented by a sign.

Reveal answer

B. Papyrus is a surface for writing. A hieroglyph is a sign that can be written on it.

Source: Assigned Egypt OER, section 6.6, “Hieroglyphics”.

Worked example

A record of goods can support a claim about exchange.

Check your understanding

  3. It made archaeology unnecessary
Reveal answer and reasoning

A. They could compare a known text with Egyptian scripts The same decree provided a basis for comparison across scripts.

9. Trade and resources

The Nile carried people and goods. Trade helped Egyptians obtain resources such as timber and metals. Egypt and Nubia exchanged goods and ideas and sometimes fought for control. Nubia had independent societies, including the Kingdom of Kush.

Worked example

A merchant who exchanges grain for timber is trading. An army that takes control of territory is conquering.

Check your understanding

Which situation best shows trade?

  1. An army takes control of a river crossing
  2. A ruler orders troops to guard a border
  3. Merchants exchange grain for timber
Reveal answer and reasoning

C. Merchants exchange grain for timber Trade is an exchange of goods or resources.

10. Compare Egypt with Mesopotamia

Cumulative review

Both societies depended on rivers and used irrigation, specialized jobs, and writing. Egypt developed along the Nile and had long periods of rule under pharaohs. Early Mesopotamia, along the Tigris and Euphrates, included independent city-states. Egyptian hieroglyphs and Mesopotamian cuneiform were different writing systems.

Worked example

A comparison can name a shared need and two solutions. Both societies needed to keep records, and each used its own writing system.

Check your understanding

Which comparison names one similarity and one difference?

  1. Both societies kept records, and they used different writing systems
  2. Both societies grew near rivers and used exactly the same writing system
  3. Egypt used irrigation, and Mesopotamia had no need to manage water
Reveal answer and reasoning

A. Both societies kept records, and they used different writing systems Recordkeeping was a shared need. The scripts differed.

11. Connect Egypt and ancient Israel

Cumulative review

Egypt appears in the Exodus narrative, which is foundational to Jewish tradition. A religious narrative and independent historical evidence answer different questions. Egyptians generally worshipped many gods. Worship of one God became central to Judaism.

The Exodus narrative helps explain a tradition’s beliefs and identity. A separate historical claim about a particular event would need evidence suited to that claim.
