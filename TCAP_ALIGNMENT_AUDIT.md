# Learn With Ry — Tennessee Grade 6 TCAP Alignment Audit

Audit date: October 6, 2026  
Build: source-locked all-subject rebuild v19

## Honest conclusion

The module is aligned to the Tennessee standards that overlap the recovered classroom materials, but it is **not yet complete full-year TCAP coverage**. “TCAP compliant” is not used as a certification claim. Tennessee's blueprint is an assessment map; Riley's teacher materials remain the curriculum source.

## ELA

The 2026–27 Grade 6 blueprint assigns approximately 32–34% of operational items to informational reading, 32–34% to literature, 13–18% to vocabulary, 13–14% to conventions, and 16 points to written expression. The Grade 6–8 overview includes selected response, constructed response, and technology-enhanced items, with two passage-based writing prompts.

Current strengths: literature/theme, point of view, context and vocabulary, tone/mood, central idea, figurative language, conventions/dialogue, task words, evidence, narrative planning/drafting/revision, and fresh Forms A–C.

Remaining full-year TCAP gaps: a blueprint-balanced informational-text bank; regular paired/multiple-passage synthesis; authentic technology-enhanced practice; and independently scored multi-paragraph writing in all three Grade 6 modes rather than narrative alone.

## Science

The 2026–27 Grade 6 blueprint contains 39–40 operational items: Physical Science 15–18%, Life Science 29–36%, Earth and Space Science 51–62%, with Engineering embedded. The assessment overview reports 48–49 total items across two online subparts and uses clusters, discrete multiple-choice/multiple-select, composites, and technology-enhanced items.

Current strengths: the teacher's Heat Transfer and Energy Resources scope; energy in systems; conduction, convection, radiation; models; thermal vocabulary; renewable/nonrenewable resources; consumption/sustainability; technology comparison; impacts; data/CER; and engineering tradeoffs. These substantially overlap 6.PS3.1–2 and 6.ESS3.1–3.

Remaining full-year TCAP gaps: the assessed ecosystem standards (6.LS2.1–5 and 6.LS4.1), the full weather/climate and water-system standards (6.ESS2.1–7), and separate, authentic cluster/composite/technology-enhanced simulations. Those topics cannot be called covered merely because energy connects to them.

## Question population and mastery

Build v19 contains 162 lessons and 1,451 selected-response questions, with a constructed-transfer prompt for every lesson. The 20 new source-locked lessons add 160 scored checks after connection/application augmentation. Science begins with the four rebuilt concept pathways before vocabulary rescue, corrections, or closed-note mastery. Every non-test lesson has at least four independently scored post-model opportunities and two explicit connection/application checks. Delayed retrieval is available after 24 hours, and first attempts are preserved.

This is sufficient as a **practice population for the recovered weekly classroom scope**, but raw volume is not proof of readiness. Mastery still requires at least 90% by objective, a successful constructed transfer, and delayed retrieval. ELA and Science readiness must also be calibrated against Riley's returned tests and item-level errors.

Used controlling sources include the Sept. 30 ELA Unit 1 study guide; the newsletters and assignments; Holt/McDougal *All Summer in a Day* source-match material; the teacher 49-question Heat Transfer and Energy Resources guide; the verified public STEMscopes/STEMscopedia unit structure; school percent materials; classroom Mesopotamia/Canaan materials; Tennessee standards, blueprints, and rubrics; and the retained verified public support links.

Every source is now labeled complete, partial, referenced-only, or unavailable in `SOURCE_LEDGER.md`; a referenced-only title is never counted as used content.

## Score-driven next evidence

The ELA 63 and Science 79 override any earlier readiness assumption. The next diagnostic source is the returned test/correction sheet or item-level score report. Each miss must be mapped to one of: vocabulary, concept, evidence/reasoning, task interpretation, reading load, conventions/calculation, or transfer. The repair path must then use a new parallel item and delayed recheck; changing an old answer does not erase the first attempt.
