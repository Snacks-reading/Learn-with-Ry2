# Sentence Types — parent key

Four structures with equal selected practice: 4 simple, 4 compound, 4 complex, 4 compound-complex. Four written tasks. Original examples; no private student records.

1. The designer measured the sleeve and adjusted the pattern.

Simple. One independent clause; the same subject performs two actions.

2. The designer measured the sleeve, and her partner adjusted the pattern.

Compound. Two independent clauses, each with its own subject and verb; no dependent clause.

3. After the designer measured the sleeve, her partner adjusted the pattern.

Complex. One dependent after clause plus one independent clause.

4. After the designer measured the sleeve, her partner adjusted the pattern, and I pinned the fabric.

Compound-complex. One dependent after clause plus two independent clauses.

5. Both the photographer and the artist arrived early.

Simple. A compound subject does not make two clauses. One independent clause.

6. The photographer arrived early; the artist brought the backdrop.

Compound. A semicolon joins two independent clauses. No dependent clause.

7. The artist brought the backdrop because we needed a plain background.

Complex. One independent clause and one dependent because clause; clause order does not change the type.

8. Because we needed a plain background, the artist brought a backdrop, and the photographer set up the lights.

Compound-complex. One dependent because clause and two independent clauses.

9. Our energetic dog ran across the yard, jumped over a branch, and fetched the ball.

Simple. Length and several verbs do not create extra clauses. Our dog is the subject of all three actions.

10. Our dog fetched the ball, but the puppy stayed beside me.

Compound. Our dog fetched the ball and the puppy stayed beside me are both independent.

11. Although the puppy was nervous, it followed the older dog.

Complex. Although the puppy was nervous is dependent; it followed the older dog is independent.

12. Although the puppy was nervous, it followed the older dog, but I stayed near the gate.

Compound-complex. One although dependent clause and two independent clauses joined by but.

13. Under the bright stage lights, Mina and Jo sang and danced.

Simple. Under the bright stage lights is a phrase, not a clause. A compound subject and predicate still form one independent clause.

14. Mina sang the opening verse, and Jo played the piano.

Compound. Two complete clauses joined by comma plus and; no dependent clause.

15. Jo played the piano while Mina sang the opening verse.

Complex. Jo played the piano is independent; while Mina sang the opening verse is dependent.

16. When the music stopped, Mina bowed, and Jo waved to the crowd.

Compound-complex. When the music stopped is dependent. Mina bowed and Jo waved to the crowd are two independent clauses.

17. Identify the type AND bracket every clause: If the bus arrives early, we will visit the gallery, and we will sketch the sculptures.

Compound-complex: [If the bus arrives early] dependent; [we will visit the gallery] independent; [we will sketch the sculptures] independent.

18. Explain why this is SIMPLE rather than compound: The students and their teacher read the article and discussed the evidence.

One independent clause. The students and their teacher is a compound subject; read and discussed share that subject. There is no second independent clause.

19. Write FOUR sentences about an art show: one simple, one compound, one complex, and one compound-complex. Label and count every clause.

Review all four independently: simple 1I/0D; compound 2+I/0D; complex 1I/1+D; compound-complex 2+I/1+D. Require complete clauses, correct meaning and punctuation. Do not mark mastery from labels alone.

20. Transform these ideas into a compound sentence, then a complex sentence, then a compound-complex sentence by adding one new complete thought: The rain stopped. We opened the outdoor display.

Possible compound: The rain stopped, so we opened the outdoor display. Complex: After the rain stopped, we opened the outdoor display. Compound-complex: After the rain stopped, we opened the outdoor display, and visitors entered the garden. Accept other correctly labeled and punctuated versions.

Each miss receives teaching plus a fresh parallel item. Writing is pending review; saving does not demonstrate mastery. Preserve earlier scores.
