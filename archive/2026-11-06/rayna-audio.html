➗ Math 6th in 6th

What Riley is learning

She remains in the regular sixth-grade course. Her binder now controls this path: the Big Eight; integers in deposit, withdrawal, debt, elevation, and change contexts; graphing and ordering rational numbers; absolute value; quadrants and points on the axes; rational-coordinate graphing; reflections across the x- and y-axes; and horizontal and vertical distance.

Memory resource

A four-quadrant coordinate song reinforces x-first, y-second and quadrant signs. Riley then turns it off and proves the skill with rational coordinates, axes, reflections, and distance.

Memorization requirement

Recognition is not mastery. Riley uses cover–recall–check in both directions and must produce all 24 Big Eight forms on blank paper without notes or choices. Mastery requires 24/24 today and again after at least one day. She also learns the repeating numerator-block pattern for denominators 9, 99, and 999 and uses known facts such as 1/8 to derive 3/8.

Application requirement

She must explain why negative-number order differs from magnitude, convert mixed rational forms before comparing, read the scale before plotting, identify a reflection axis from the coordinate that changed sign, and calculate axis-aligned distance as an absolute difference. The new independent check contains 40 mixed binder-matched questions.

Ask her

  • Can you rebuild the complete Big Eight table without looking?
  • Why is −12/25 greater than −0.51?
  • Which coordinate changes across each reflection axis?
  • How do you know whether to subtract x-values or y-values for distance?

🔬 Science

Teacher-source sequence

Riley now learns every visible StudyMate term before testing: abiotic and biotic; adaptation; biome and biosphere; organism, population, community, ecosystem; consumers, producers, decomposers, food chains, food webs, and energy pyramids; predator and prey; mutualism, commensalism, parasitism, and host; niche, limiting factor, carrying capacity, native species, and invasive species.

Memory resource

The Mr. Parr Ecology Song reinforces ecology, competition, and symbiosis. Riley then turns it off and applies exact vocabulary to food webs, limiting factors, and ecosystem change.

Textbook model

The five levels are organism → population → community → ecosystem → biosphere. In the salt marsh, cordgrass, birds, crabs, snails, algae, and microbes are biotic. Water, light, sediment, oxygen, salinity, and temperature are abiotic. A change in one part can spread through feeding, shelter, and resource relationships.

Ask her

  • What is added at each organization level?
  • Who benefits, is harmed, or is unaffected?
  • What evidence supports your food-web prediction?
