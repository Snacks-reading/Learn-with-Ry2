# Friday sentence quiz — parent key

22 new items: 16 selected checks and 6 written applications. Exact test questions are unavailable. Compound-complex is marked extension. Readiness requires independent evidence and separately reviewed writing; no score is inferred.

1. The designer measured the sleeve.

The designer is the subject; measured is the verb. The thought is complete. Because starts an unfinished dependent thought; the other choices are phrases.

2. Although the kitten was tiny

Although makes a subject-and-verb group depend on a main clause. Under the warm blanket has no subject-verb pair and is a phrase.

3. Simple

One subject, Maya, performs two actions. And joins verbs here, not two independent clauses.

4. Compound

Maya sketched a jacket and Leo chose the fabric can each stand alone. There is no dependent clause.

5. Complex

Although Maya liked the silk is dependent; she chose cotton is independent. The opening dependent clause needs a following comma.

6. The storm arrived, so the team went inside.

Use a comma BEFORE the coordinating conjunction so between these complete clauses.

7. Before the curtain rose, Nia checked the lights.

The entire opening dependent clause ends at rose. Place the comma there, not after the first word.

8. Nia checked the lights before the curtain rose.

An essential dependent time clause following the main clause usually needs no comma. Never separate the subject from its verb.

9. The path flooded, so we took another route.

The original has a comma splice. Comma plus so joins two complete clauses and shows the result; because alone leaves a fragment.

10. Because the dog was tired, it rested.

The correct answer has a dependent because clause and an independent clause. The final option is grammatical but simple: a shared subject performs two actions.

11. Because

Buying only a small piece is a result of the expense, so because states the reason. Although would suggest an unexpected contrast instead. Read meaning, not just sentence shape.

12. Although

Continuing despite heavy rain is unexpected. Although expresses that contrast; because would claim the rain caused the continuation.

13. After the artist mixed the colors

Include the dependent marker, subject and full predicate. The second clause is the independent thought.

14. It is simple because one subject performs both actions.

Count complete clauses, not and words or verbs. Chased its toy has no separate subject; both actions share the dog.

15. The gate was locked; we used the side entrance.

A semicolon can join closely related independent clauses. Both sides must be complete thoughts in this pattern.

16. Compound-complex

After the bell rang is dependent. I packed my bag and Jo closed the window are two independent clauses. This is an extension to help avoid misclassifying sentences from readings.

17. Combine with but and explain the comma: The pattern looked easy. The seam was difficult.

The pattern looked easy, but the seam was difficult. Two independent clauses; but shows contrast.

18. Combine with because. Put the dependent clause FIRST: We moved indoors. Rain soaked the field.

Because rain soaked the field, we moved indoors. Opening dependent clause ends at field; comma follows.

19. Rewrite your previous sentence with the independent clause FIRST. Explain punctuation.

We moved indoors because rain soaked the field. No comma is needed before this essential reason clause.

20. Repair this fragment in your own words and identify both clauses: Although the project was difficult.

Example: Although the project was difficult, we finished it. Dependent: Although the project was difficult. Independent: we finished it.

21. Write one simple sentence with two actions and one compound sentence about an animal. Explain the difference.

Require one shared subject with two predicates in the simple sentence; two independently complete clauses in the compound sentence. Check punctuation and meaning separately.

22. Fresh transfer: Write three connected sentences about preparing a fashion display: one simple, one compound and one complex. Label every clause and justify each comma.

Review: three correctly classified sentences; correct clause labels; comma before FANBOYS between complete clauses; comma after an opening dependent clause; coherent meanings. Saving is not an automatic correct mark.

For each written response: check clause structure, punctuation, intended relationship and explanation. Any unresolved error receives a new example and teaching. Do not overwrite the first response.
