## Source review before answering or building instruction

Check every identified relevant source in this order before making instructional claims or authoring a lesson or assessment.

1. Teacher newsletter and uploaded materials, including handwritten annotations and teacher rubrics.
2. Schoology references, every published course folder, underlying classroom assignments and linked resources. Inventory a listing and reading its contents are separate actions.
3. The district-adopted textbook and exact assigned pages. Confirm adoption and edition. An alternative public text is supplemental unless a classroom match is verified.
4. Current Tennessee standards and rubrics, used to detect gaps and appropriate rigor.
5. External teaching resources, including previously sourced material in the resource register, source ledger and prior project files.

Do not replace an original with an earlier generated parent guide, summary, objective list or lesson. Recover source content through existing uploads, authentic classroom access and public originals. A working URL or successful HTTP response does not establish content review. Reuse an existing signed-in tab and request sign-in only for the actual blocked screen.

Record each source's identity, edition/pages, assignment, source type, current access, what was read, relevant objectives, review date, limitations and media playback status. Use distinct states for identified, page opened, partially read, completely read, interaction tested and unavailable. Review each newly uploaded source before deciding it is a duplicate. Retain duplicate provenance without multiplying objectives.

Map every source requirement to teaching, modeling, guided practice, independent application, assessment and retention. Include diagram labels, vocabulary, figures, dates, people, places, questions and directions. Reconcile factual conflicts explicitly. The assigned OER social pyramid places soldiers and scribes together on tier 3. Repair any assessment using a conflicting tier.

Incomplete controlling sources prevent a complete-source certification and prevent the affected new assessments from being released. Interface and governance improvements may be published while instructional content remains in a clearly marked review area. The parent sees exact blockers and next actions. Do not represent a source review as finished based on lesson counts, question totals or keyword checks.

## Learning sequence

Every released lesson follows this sequence.

1. Teach. Explain the idea and vocabulary in short readable chunks. Show a diagram, organizer, model or appropriate representation. Connect the prerequisite, current use and future application.
2. Guided practice. Model the thinking explicitly, then let Ry respond before feedback. Teach why plausible alternatives fail.
3. Independent application. Use a fresh context with no hints or exposed explanation before the response.
4. Assessment. Use multiple distinct questions, sufficient coverage per objective and verified answer keys. Rotate answer positions and question order without breaking passage dependencies or saved item identities.
5. Retention. Schedule distinct retrieval after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days. Each is a separate attempt. Passive listening and completion alone do not establish mastery.

Include organizers, interactive models, diagrams, card sorts, map reconstruction, worked examples, mnemonic cues and recall practice wherever they improve a specific objective. Search for the exact teacher-used song/video first. Give each external resource a concrete student task and a closed-resource recall/application step. Check factual accuracy, sixth-grade fit, accessibility and omissions. HHMI activities that target older learners require explicit sixth-grade adaptation. Do not treat simulation behavior or media playback as verified from a text listing.

Only ELA and Math have write-in prompts. Science, Social Studies and Theater use multiple choice for independent assessment. Adapt classroom writing prompts into multiple choice for these subjects while preserving the skill. Map questions use a precise verified classroom map, accurate labeling and consistent geography. Do not improvise misleading map geometry.

## Narration

Provide controls for Read this lesson, Read question and choices, and Read writing/review prompt for ELA and Math. Provide Pause, Resume, Stop, and Slow, Learning pace and Regular speeds. Prefer the best natural available English voice and permit an explicit choice. Report when audio cannot start. Device voice quality must be checked on the actual phone before calling it verified.

Read only the currently visible lesson, prompt and choices. Do not include hidden answers, explanations, worked answers for an unanswered check, or parent notes. Spoken feedback is available only after Ry submits her answer. Stop narration whenever changing lessons, stages, questions, subject or navigation destination. Ignore stale speech callbacks after a change. Preserve current reading position for a pause within the same narration. A navigation change starts a new narration context.
